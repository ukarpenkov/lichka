## Text

```
Lichka is a private place to talk to yourself — in chats, not documents.

Open a chat for work. Another for ideas. One for the grocery list you keep rewriting at 11pm. Each thread keeps its own history, avatar, and pace. You write the way you already think: short lines, voice notes, a photo when words are slower than a glance.

No account. No feed. No server watching. Everything lives on your phone.

————————

FOUR WAYS TO SEND

Every message starts in the chat composer. You choose how it should behave:

• Note — lands in the thread right away
• Reminder — a push at the time you set; until then it sits in Scheduled
• Alarm — full-screen over the lock screen when you must not miss it
• Periodic — repeats on your interval; each fire arrives as a notification

Same chat. Different timing. The message stays tied to the conversation it belongs to — tap a notification and you land on that exact line.

————————

VOICE, IMAGES, SEARCH

Hold to record a voice note (up to 60 seconds). Play it back in the thread. Attach a photo when a screenshot or a shelf says more than a paragraph. Edit text or time later — the message keeps an “edited” mark. Search across every chat, or dig inside one thread when you only remember a half-phrase.

————————

SCHEDULED — AND A WIDGET

The Scheduled tab lists every active reminder, alarm, and repeating item, sorted by when it fires. Open any of them and jump straight into the chat. Put the list on your home screen with the Android widget so the next thing you owe yourself is visible without unlocking the app.

————————

LOOKS LIKE A TERMINAL, FEELS ALIVE

Lichka’s interface is intentionally sparse: pixel titles, monospace lines, a stream that reads like a personal CLI session — not rounded bubbles chasing a messenger clone.

Thirteen two-color themes. Soft light. Pure black. Green-on-black if you want the classic terminal mood. Switch until the screen matches how you work at night or at a desk.

Motion is quiet but present — messages ease in, lists settle, gestures respond. Haptics and sounds are on by default; turn either off in Settings if you prefer silence.

————————

YOUR DATA, YOUR COPIES

Chats, media, and settings stay in local storage. When you want a safety net, export a ZIP archive of data and media, or upload a backup to Google Drive by hand. Import later in merge or replace mode. No automatic cloud sync in the background — you decide when a copy leaves the device.

Russian and English follow the system language.

————————

WHO IT’S FOR

If notes apps feel like filing cabinets, and messengers feel like rooms full of other people, Lichka sits in between: thematic chats with yourself, with time built in — reminders, alarms, and repeats that belong to the same conversation as the thought that started them.

Install it. Make one chat. Send yourself something you won’t want to lose.
```
